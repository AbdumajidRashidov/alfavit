# Three 30-second clips, October 2026

Screen recording and captions only: no voice, no face, no music needed. Each clip is recorded once with the site in Uzbek and gets two caption tracks, Uzbek and Russian, so you post it twice. The Russian captions describe actions instead of quoting button labels, because the screen shows the Uzbek ones.

**Format.** Vertical 9:16 (1080×1920) for Instagram Reels and Telegram. Captions sit in the lower third, white on a dark band, one line at a time, at most 42 characters each. Turn on Focus / Do Not Disturb before you record.

**Links.** Telegram posts carry the link in the text. Instagram does not make links in a caption clickable: put it in the bio or on a story link sticker, and say "link in bio" in the post text. Russian posts link the `/ru/` version of the page.

**What not to claim.** Only what the site does today. The desktop apps convert as you type, but nothing here should promise that a web address typed in the desktop app is left alone; keep addresses out of the desktop app on camera.

## Clip 1 — Typing Ö, Ğ, Ş, Ç on a phone

**Device:** iPhone (an Android phone with Gboard works the same way; the menu names differ). Record with Control Center → Screen Recording.

**Before you record:** remove the Turkish keyboard if it is already added, so you can show adding it.

**Steps**

1. Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Add New Keyboard → Türkçe → Türkçe Q.
2. Back in Settings → General → Keyboard, turn **Auto-Correction** off. Hold on the switch for a beat.
3. Open Notes. Tap the globe key until the Turkish keyboard shows.
4. Type `özbek ğalaba şahar çoy`. For the first letter, long-press **o** and slide to **ö** so the long-press is visible; type the rest from the keys on the keyboard (ğ, ş and ç have their own keys).
5. Switch to Safari and open `alfavit.uz/guide/keyboard`. Scroll to the iPhone and Android section and stop.

These four words have no **i** on purpose: on the Turkish keyboard the key where i usually sits types a dotless **ı**, and the dotted i is the key to the right of ş. Check that on your phone before recording, and drop caption 5 if your keyboard differs.

## Clip 2 — Converting a Word document

**Device:** Mac, Chrome, site in Uzbek. Make the window narrow and tall (about 900×1300) so it fills a vertical frame. Record with ⇧⌘5 → Record Selected Portion.

**Before you record:** make `Buyruq.docx` in Word with three things on screen at once: a bold heading `ТОШКЕНТ ШАҲАР ҲОКИМЛИГИ`, a paragraph `Ўзбекистон Республикаси Президентининг қарори`, and a small two-column table with `Мактаб ўқувчилари учун ғалаба куни` in one cell. Put it on the Desktop.

**Steps**

1. Open `alfavit.uz/files`.
2. Click the dashed box (**Fayl tanlang (.txt, .srt, .docx)**) and pick `Buyruq.docx`. The box shows the file name. (The box opens a file picker; dragging a file onto it does not work, so click.)
3. Click **Oʻgirish va yuklab olish**. Wait for **Tayyor — yuklab olish boshlanadi.**
4. Open `Buyruq (yangi lotin).docx` from Downloads in Word, next to the original.
5. Hold for three seconds on the result. It reads `TOŞKENT ŞAHAR HOKIMLIGI`, `Özbekiston Respublikasi Prezidentining qarori` and `Maktab öquvçilari uçun ğalaba kuni`, with the bold and the table where they were.

## After posting

In Cloudflare Web Analytics → Paths, the visits show with their UTM query strings, so `utm_source` says whether Telegram or Instagram sent them. In Analytics Engine the landing `pageview` carries the three UTM values (blob4–blob6). Only clip 2 lands on a page that converts: a file converted on /files carries the UTM values too. Clip 1 and clip 3 land on pages without a converter, and a conversion after the visitor moves on carries none, so tie it back to the landing `pageview` through the same-day session hash (blob9). Add the numbers to `metrics.md` a week after the last post.
